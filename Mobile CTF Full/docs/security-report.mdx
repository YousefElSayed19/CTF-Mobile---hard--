# Security report — NexaWallet

Training documentation for a fictional, self-contained Android
reverse-engineering lab. Built around "NexaWallet," a fictional digital
wallet app. No real users, accounts, funds, or backend exist anywhere in
this lab.

## Objective

Dynamic Android instrumentation practice, rated **hard**, expected solve
time 45–90 minutes for a player comfortable with jadx and basic Frida
scripting.

## Starting point

The player installs the APK fresh, registers a local account (no real
data, no network calls to any real service), and reaches the Transfer
screen showing a fixed EGP 100.00 limit.

## Attack chain

1. **Recon:** Static decompilation (jadx) of `TransferActivity`,
   `IntegrityGate`, `TransferAuthorizer`, and `ReviewState` reveals the
   full shape of both gates and the flag-minting call site, without
   revealing the flag itself (which is obfuscated, not plaintext).
2. **Gate 1 bypass — diagnosing a hook that doesn't fire:** A direct hook
   on `IntegrityGate.isTampered()` installs cleanly but has no observable
   effect, because ART/JIT inlines the small function into its caller.
   The player must recognize this specific failure mode and pivot to
   hooking the underlying framework methods (`Socket.connect`,
   `BufferedReader.readLine`, `Thread.getName`) instead.
3. **Gate 2 discovery and reach:** `ReviewGateActivity` is present in the
   compiled app and declared in the manifest, but `exported="false"` and
   never referenced by any other component — unreachable through the UI
   or `adb shell am start`. The player must launch it from inside the
   running (Frida-attached) process via the app's own `Context`.
4. **Flag collection:** With both gates satisfied, submitting an
   over-limit transfer causes `TransferAuthorizer.mintClearanceToken()`
   to execute and display the `duck{...}` token.

## Mapped concepts

This lab is not built around a CWE/OWASP-Mobile-Top-10 single finding in
the way a typical injection or authz bug is; it's a demonstration of a
broader architectural pattern — **client-side-only authorization and
proof-of-authorization** — closest to OWASP Mobile Top 10 M8 (Security
Misconfiguration) and M9 (Insecure Data Storage/client-trust issues) in
spirit, and directly analogous to "never trust the client" findings
common in real mobile-banking and anti-cheat security reviews.

## Remediation (summary — full detail in `remediation.mdx`)

Move the authorization decision, the integrity-signal evaluation, the
review/approval workflow, and the proof-of-success minting all to a
backend the attacker does not control; treat every client-side check in
this lab as, at most, a signal reported to that backend, never as a
decision made on its own.

## Verification

Retests for a remediated real-world design should confirm: no amount of
client-side manipulation (hooking, patching, direct memory/field
modification) can produce a valid transaction confirmation without the
backend independently verifying and issuing it. For this lab itself
(unchanged, since it is intentionally vulnerable for training purposes),
verification is: the documented solve chain reliably works end-to-end on
a clean install, and a clean device/emulator with no instrumentation
attached is never blocked below the legitimate EGP 100 limit and never
produces the flag through the normal, unmodified UI flow.

## Scope

Local, player-supplied (BYOD) environment only. No real accounts, data,
funds, or backend services exist anywhere in this lab or its supporting
documentation. See `../documentation/Public Briefing.md` for the
player-facing, non-spoiler description, and `official-solution.mdx` for
the full spoiler-laden writeup.
